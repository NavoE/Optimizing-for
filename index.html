Chapter Three

What Are We
Actually
Optimizing
For?

We talk about productivity, efficiency, and shareholder value. But economies are not abstract systems. They are made up of people.

I studied Urban Planning — a discipline that sits at the intersection of the physical, economic, and social. Very quickly, you realize none of those pieces function in isolation. A city, or a country, can be well designed on paper. But if people do not have access to stable livelihoods, the system does not hold in practice.

Reduced consumption
Reduced innovation
Reduced long-term returns

At a basic level — who sustains demand?
This is where the contradiction becomes harder to ignore.

Chapter Five

Growth Doesn't
Come From
Survival Mode.

The creativity, innovation, and risk-taking that drive real economic growth do not come from desperation. They come from stability. From people having enough security to think, build, and imagine beyond their immediate needs.

This is where I keep coming back to Maslow. Work, markets, and economic systems should not exist solely to drive profit. At their best, they create the conditions for people to move beyond survival — to have choice, dignity, and the ability to fully participate.

If our systems are not creating those conditions, then we are not actually optimizing for growth.
We are constraining it.
